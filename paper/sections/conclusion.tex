\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We predicted next-day wildfire spread with a per-pixel model and 23 engineered spatial features.
Tuned XGBoost reaches a test PR AUC of 0.257 (95\% bootstrap interval over fire events 0.19--0.33),
against 0.106 for persistence. The random forest (0.225) and CNN (0.284) of Huot et al.\ both lie
inside our interval, so our model sits between them, but we make no claim of a significant
difference.

The signal is spatial. Removing the fire-neighbourhood features drops PR AUC from 0.248 to 0.048,
while tuning moves it by only about 0.01. Training on 5\% of the training patches gives almost the
same eval PR AUC (0.204 against 0.207), so the model is limited by bias, not variance: more data
or tuning will not help much.

The model finds fire at the existing front, with recall of 0.96--0.99 within 1\,px of day-$t$ fire,
but recall is near zero beyond 5\,km, where 64\% of the missed fire pixels lie. It also raises
false alarms around fires that are dying down. Both errors come from the per-pixel design, which
sees the patch only through fixed-radius summaries.

Finally, we found that the patches are stored south-up, which the dataset does not state. Every
direction-dependent feature, such as fire upwind, depends on this orientation.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

Phase~2 will address the limits found above.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Whole-patch models.} A U-Net~\cite{ronneberger2015} or CNN that sees the whole patch
  can learn the shape of the fire front instead of a fixed-radius halo.
\item \textbf{Imbalance-aware losses.} Focal loss~\cite{lin2017} or a class-weighted segmentation
  loss can replace negative subsampling.
\item \textbf{Multi-day inputs.} Several days of fire history, as in WildfireSpreadTS~\cite{gerard2023},
  may help tell growing fires from dying ones.
\item \textbf{Split leakage.} The dataset has no fire identifier, and the test split alone contains
  40 exact duplicate patches. We will check whether one fire appears in more than one split.
\item \textbf{Uncertain labels.} Cloud and smoke pixels ($-1$) are now ignored; modelling them, for
  example by filling them from neighbouring days, would use more of the data.
\end{itemize}
