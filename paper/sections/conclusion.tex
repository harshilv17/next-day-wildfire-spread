\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We predicted next-day wildfire spread with a per-pixel model and 23 engineered spatial features.
Tuned XGBoost reaches a test PR AUC of 0.257 (95\% bootstrap interval over fire events 0.19--0.33),
against 0.106 for persistence. The random forest (0.225) and CNN (0.284) of Huot et al.\ both lie
inside our interval, so our model sits between them, but we make no claim of a significant
difference.

The signal is spatial. Removing the fire-neighbourhood features drops PR AUC from 0.248 to 0.048,
while tuning moves it by only about 0.01. Training on 5\% of the training patches gives almost the
same eval PR AUC (0.204 against 0.207), so the model is limited by bias, not variance: more data
or tuning will not help much.

The model finds fire at the existing front, with recall of 0.96--0.99 within 1\,px of day-$t$ fire,
but recall is near zero beyond 5\,km, where 64\% of the missed fire pixels lie. It also raises
false alarms around fires that are dying down. Both errors come from the per-pixel design, which
sees the patch only through fixed-radius summaries.

Finally, we found that the patches are stored south-up, which the dataset does not state. Every
direction-dependent feature, such as fire upwind, depends on this orientation.

Phase~2 confirms that the missing ingredient was spatial context. A U-Net that sees $44\times44$\,km
reaches a test PR AUC of 0.275 $\pm$ 0.008 over three seeds against 0.255 $\pm$ 0.002 for XGBoost, and
it is ahead for every seed when both are scored on the same fires, by 0.012 to 0.027. A CNN that sees only
$9\times9$\,km, about the reach of our neighbourhood features, ties XGBoost. Unlike XGBoost, the U-Net
also uses more training data, up to about half of the training set.

The smaller choices matter much less. Focal loss beats weighted BCE by about 0.007, and none of the flips
and rotations we tried helps; rotations are even slightly worse on test. Correct augmentation must turn
the wind direction with the patch, and on a south-up grid a 90-degree turn of the array adds $90^\circ$ to
it. These differences are of the same size as the noise of training itself: a second run with the same
seed on the same GPU moves the score by up to 0.02. Results for neural networks on this dataset should
therefore be reported as means over seeds, as we do.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

Several limits remain.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hybrid model.} Giving the U-Net the XGBoost probability as a 13th input channel, computed
  out-of-fold on train, may combine the engineered features with learned spatial context.
\item \textbf{Calibrated probabilities.} Focal loss~\cite{lin2017} flattens the U-Net's probabilities;
  a recalibration fitted on eval is needed before they are read as fire risk.
\item \textbf{Multi-day inputs.} Several days of fire history, as in WildfireSpreadTS~\cite{gerard2023},
  may help tell growing fires from dying ones.
\item \textbf{Location-disjoint splits.} 30\% of test patches share their location with a training
  patch, and these are easier for every model. A split that keeps locations or fire events apart
  would give a stricter test.
\item \textbf{Uncertain labels.} Cloud and smoke pixels ($-1$) are now ignored; modelling them, for
  example by filling them from neighbouring days, would use more of the data.
\end{itemize}
